# On-demand / app-based ride-hailing platforms in London for a TfL PCO driver with a 2023 Skoda Superb Estate iV (as of 2026-11-24)

Method note: Research was done with web search only. Direct page fetches of bolt.eu, driver.free-now.com and blackties.co.uk were blocked by this session's network allowlist (DNS failure), so the facts attributed to those URLs come from search-result snippets and summaries of those pages, not full-page reads. Treat model-list details (exact Superb listing) as needing confirmation in each app's own eligibility checker. Taxi-Point (taxi-point.co.uk) and official sites were the main trade/primary sources.

## Q1. Baseline: TfL licensing / ZEC status of the Superb Estate iV and cross-platform rules (2026)

### Takeaway
The car is already a licensed PHV, so the 2023 ZEC rule for first-time licensing is already satisfied; the binding constraints now are TfL's 10-year age cap at re-licensing and each platform's own (stricter) age/model lists. A new 20% VAT treatment on London PHV operator fares from 2 Jan 2026 affects payouts across platforms.

### Cited Findings
- Since 1 Jan 2023 all PHVs licensed by TfL for the first time must be zero-emission capable (ZEC); a plug-in hybrid qualifies with either <=50 g/km CO2 and >=10 miles zero-emission range, or <=75 g/km and >=20 miles — [Hertz PHV ZEC guide 2026](https://www.hertz.co.uk/p/hertz-together/phv-zec-requirement-for-first-time-licences-from-1-jan-2023-the-2026-fleet-planning-reality-for-pco-drivers)
- Existing PHVs do not need to be ZEC but must be no older than 10 years at re-licensing — [Hertz PHV ZEC guide](https://www.hertz.co.uk/p/hertz-together/phv-zec-requirement-for-first-time-licences-from-1-jan-2023-the-2026-fleet-planning-reality-for-pco-drivers)
- V5C certificates rarely show zero-emission range; drivers may need a manufacturer evidence document at licensing — [Hertz PHV ZEC guide](https://www.hertz.co.uk/p/hertz-together/phv-zec-requirement-for-first-time-licences-from-1-jan-2023-the-2026-fleet-planning-reality-for-pco-drivers)
- TfL's 2025 Taxi & Private Hire Action Plan references a 1 January 2033 target for zero-emission-capable fleet — [TfL Taxi and private hire action plan 2025 (PDF)](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/taxi-private-hire-action-plan-2025.pdf)
- TfL (Oct 2026) set out an ambition/consultation for all newly licensed PHVs to be fully zero-emission (not merely ZEC) from July 2028 (WAVs from July 2030) — a proposal, not adopted rule — [Wired-Gov, TfL announcement Oct 2026](https://wired-gov.net/wg/news.nsf/articles/TfL+sets+out+ambition+for+all+new+private+hire+vehicles+to+be+zeroemission+within+five+years+23102026112000?open=); summarised in [Hertz guide](https://www.hertz.co.uk/p/hertz-together/phv-zec-requirement-for-first-time-licences-from-1-jan-2023-the-2026-fleet-planning-reality-for-pco-drivers)
- From 2 January 2026 VAT on private hire journeys by London operators is 20% of the passenger fare, with the remainder split between operator and driver (stated by Wheely; affects all London operators acting as principal) — [Wheely driver FAQ: Statements of Earnings and VAT](https://wheely.com/en/faq-for-drivers/uk/2424662/4197280); a separate secondary source also reports VAT applying to the full fare from 2 Jan 2026 — [PCO GO, Uber vs Bolt 2026](https://www.pcogo.co.uk/uber-vs-bolt-in-2026-which-platform-pays-more-for-london-pco-drivers/)
- London had 1,843 PHV operator licences in force on 1 April 2026 (third-party summary of DfT data) — [Taxidex London TfL](https://taxidex.com/taxi-licensing/london-tfl)

### Inferences
- A 2023 Superb iV (WLTP electric range in the ~30+ mile class, CO2 well under 50 g/km per Skoda's published figures — not verified in this session) comfortably meets ZEC; the July 2028 zero-emission proposal applies to newly licensed vehicles, so it should not affect this already-licensed car, which can be re-licensed until roughly 2033 under the 10-year cap.
- The 2033 date and the 10-year cap coincide, so the car's TfL working life is roughly to 2033 regardless; platform tiers (Uber Comfort, Bolt Comfort) will drop it earlier.

### Gaps
- Exact Superb Estate iV WLTP CO2/EV-range figures not retrieved from a source in this session.
- Final outcome of TfL's Oct 2026 zero-emission consultation not found.

## Q2. Bolt (standard, Comfort, XL, Business, Scheduled rides)

### Takeaway
Bolt operates in London and is the strongest "second app" for this car: standard Bolt definitely, Bolt Comfort very likely (London Comfort = 2017+ registration), not XL (needs 6 passenger seats). Bolt has a London scheduled-rides feature where drivers can see and accept trips in advance, and it accepts the driver's own hire & reward policy.

### Cited Findings
- Operating: Bolt has a London driver earnings page and London document requirements — [Bolt London driver page](https://bolt.eu/en/driver/earn/united-kingdom/london/); [Bolt London document requirements](https://bolt.eu/en-gb/support/articles/360009722719/)
- Standard Bolt (UK): four doors, four passenger seats excluding driver, no commercial branding; Bolt does not provide vehicles — [Bolt: Requirements for Bolt category in the UK](https://bolt.eu/en-gb/support/articles/4414409664402/)
- Bolt Comfort (London): vehicle registered 2017 or newer; four doors; >=4 passenger seats; no commercial branding; current local licence; available in London and 11 other UK cities — [Bolt: Requirements for Comfort category in the UK](https://bolt.eu/en/support/articles/4414548771858/)
- Bolt XL (UK): four doors, minimum 6 passenger seats excluding driver, registration 2013+, selected areas only — [Bolt: Requirements for XL category](https://bolt.eu/en-gb/support/articles/4414541025298/)
- No evidence found of a Bolt "Executive" category in the UK — [search summary of Bolt categories pages, e.g. Bolt Rides Categories](https://bolt.eu/en-ch/driver/guide/categories/)
- Insurance: London insurance certificate must be valid, name the vehicle VRM, and explicitly state "Hire and Reward" or "Private Hire"; driver must be policyholder or named as entitled to drive; fleet-insurance schedules accepted for hired cars — [Bolt London document requirements](https://bolt.eu/en-gb/support/articles/360009722719/); [DM News Bolt driver requirements UK](https://dmnews.co.uk/bolt-driver-requirements-uk-licence-vehicle-documents-getting-started/)

- Scheduled rides (driver side): drivers can view and accept scheduled trips in advance via a "New scheduled requests" banner before going online; accepted rides listed under Menu > Scheduled requests > Accepted, with fixed price and pickup time; drivers asked to accept only rides they intend to complete — [Bolt London driver page](https://bolt.eu/en/driver/earn/united-kingdom/london/); [Bolt driver guide: Scheduled Rides](https://bolt.eu/en-sa/driver/guide/schedule-rides/) (note: guide is a non-UK locale page)

- Commission: secondary sources put Bolt's take at ~15–20% depending on location/performance, vs ~25% for Uber; no booking fee charged to riders on PHV — [PCO GO, Uber vs Bolt 2026](https://www.pcogo.co.uk/uber-vs-bolt-in-2026-which-platform-pays-more-for-london-pco-drivers/); [Fleeto Uber vs Bolt 2026](https://fleeto.co.uk/blog/uber-vs-bolt-price-comparison-for-drivers/); [Westgate Hire Bolt vs Uber](https://westgatehire.com/blog/bolt-vs-uber-guide-for-london-drivers/)

## Q3. FREENOW (now "Freenow by Lyft") and Gett (Lyft) — status in London

Freenow is operating in London in 2026 under the "Freenow by Lyft" brand; it runs PHV (incl. a Comfort tier) alongside its core black-cab business. Gett (acquired by Lyft, April 2026) remains a black-cab-only driver platform; its new PHV option is fulfilled by Freenow PHV drivers — so a PCO driver's route to both is Freenow.
