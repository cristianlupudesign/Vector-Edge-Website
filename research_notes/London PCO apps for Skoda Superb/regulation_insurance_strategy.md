## 1. TfL rules: multiple operators, booking via a licensed operator, cross-border hiring, and becoming your own (small) operator

### Takeaway
A London PHV driver may only carry out bookings received from a TfL-licensed London PHV operator; nothing found in TfL guidance prohibits registering with several operators (Uber, Bolt, FREENOW, etc.), and multi-apping is industry-normal (Bolt says ~9 in 10 drivers are on multiple platforms). Taking private/direct clients legally requires the bookings to go through a licensed operator — either an existing one or the driver's own TfL operator licence (reported at ~£2,000 for a 5-year licence in the 0–10 vehicle band, plus an operating centre with planning evidence). VAT is the big complication: London operators must act as principal and account for VAT on the full fare, but a one-car operator below the £90,000 taxable-turnover threshold need not register.

### Inferences
- Multi-operator working is lawful in London so long as each journey is booked via, and recorded by, a licensed operator; the driver must not accept street hails or arrange trips directly with a passenger ("touting"/plying for hire). "Cross-border hiring" (out-of-area licensing) is irrelevant for a TfL-licensed driver in a TfL-licensed vehicle working for TfL-licensed operators, but a TfL driver/vehicle must not take bookings from a non-London operator (licensing triad: operator, driver and vehicle from the same licensing authority).
- To take private/airport/account clients directly the driver has three legal routes: (a) pass them through an existing licensed operator (e.g. a small local firm or an account-work operator); (b) obtain their own TfL operator licence (smallest band, ~£2,000/5 yrs if third-party figure holds) with an operating centre that has planning permission/lawful use evidence, record-keeping of every booking (date, time, pickup, destination, driver, vehicle, fare), complaints/lost-property procedures, and public liability insurance; (c) for one-car operators, the operating centre can sometimes be a home address but planning evidence is still required — confirm with TfL and the local planning authority.
- A one-car own-operator in London would be a principal supplier, so all fares count toward the VAT threshold; at ~£90k/yr of own-operator fares they would have to charge 20% VAT. Below that, no VAT is due — a meaningful price advantage over Uber/Bolt (who must add VAT in London since Jan 2026). Turnover from Uber/Bolt trips (where Uber is principal and pays the driver) is generally not the driver's VATable supply in London — confirm with an accountant.

## 2. Vehicle compliance: ZEC status, age limits, ULEZ, Congestion Charge, airport charges

### Takeaway
Since 1 Jan 2023 every PHV licensed for the first time in London must be Zero Emission Capable (ZEC); a 2023 Superb iV that is already licensed can be relicensed until the 10-year age limit (i.e. until ~2033 from first registration), per current rules. TfL is consulting (closes 2 Dec 2026) on requiring new-to-licensing PHVs to be fully zero-emission from July 2028 (WAVs 2030), which would not affect an already-licensed car but would matter for its replacement. The Congestion Charge is £18/day from 2 Jan 2026; PHVs are not exempt (except WAVs on a booking), and the 100% Cleaner Vehicle Discount ended — electric cars now get 25% off (£13.50) on Auto Pay; a plug-in hybrid like the Superb iV pays the full £18. Airport drop-off charges in 2026: Heathrow £7/entry, Gatwick ~£10, Stansted £10 (15 min), Luton £7–£9 (disputed), London City £8 (5 min).

## 3. Insurance for multi-operator work

### Takeaway
Mainstream PHV insurers (Zego, INSHUR, Cover My Taxi) state that one private-hire (hire & reward) policy covers work across Uber, Bolt, FREENOW and local operators, and certificates can be uploaded to each platform; drivers should still check their schedule for any named-operator restriction. Operators must hold their own public liability insurance; the driver's motor policy is separate.

## 4. Multi-apping strategy, risks, and earnings data

### Takeaway
Multi-apping is mainstream in London (Bolt: ~90% of drivers). The standard routine is: be online on several apps, accept one job, immediately go offline on the others. Evidence on earnings comes mainly from the June 2025 Oxford/Worker Info Exchange study: Uber UK drivers' inflation-adjusted hourly income fell from over £22 to just over £19 (before costs) after dynamic pricing, while Uber's average take rose from ~25% to ~29% (sometimes >50%). Rival commissions are lower (Bolt ~15–20%, FREENOW ~15–18% per third-party guides). Reliable 2026 per-platform hourly data was not found.

## 5. Advance-booking features across London platforms

### Takeaway
Uber Reserve lets drivers see and accept reservation requests up to 7 days ahead (Driver app: Opportunities → Reservations), with fare, pickup and drop-off shown before accepting; eligibility depends on following the reservations policy. Riders can book 30 minutes to 30 days ahead. No reliable driver-side information on Bolt or FREENOW pre-booking visibility was found.
